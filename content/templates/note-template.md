**Optimal structure per note**

- **Subheadings:** 3–5. Anything beyond 6 makes scanning slow. Let RAME be your scaffold; not every section needs a heading.
- **Tags:** 2–4 max. One drug class or symptom domain (e.g. `antidepressants`), one clinical context (`symptom-management`), one RAME phase if you want that granularity. Tag sprawl kills retrieval — more tags means less signal per tag.
- **Categories:** 1 primary. Maybe 1 secondary if it genuinely lives in two domains. Don't over-categorize to compensate for vague content.

The note should be scannable in under 30 seconds by someone who already knows the topic but needs a specific detail. That's the test.

**Notion → Obsidian pipeline vs direct Obsidian**

The two-step pipeline has a real cost (time, friction, risk of never finishing the condensation) but also a real benefit: the compression step is retrieval practice. You're not just copying — you're reconstructing from memory and judgment, which is the best single thing you can do for retention.

Practical split:

- **1h video:** Notion first. You can't write a polished clinical note while listening. Dump into Notion, then compress into Obsidian afterward. That compression step is the learning.
- **Short paper (≤15 pages):** Direct Obsidian. Read, highlight mentally, write the note. One step.
- **Long review or guideline:** Notion or even just highlights in a PDF reader, then distill.

The pipeline only works if you protect the compression step. Schedule it immediately after, not "later." Notes sitting in Notion are a liability.

**Knowledge retention specifically**

The biggest lever is active reconstruction during the Notion → Obsidian step — close your Notion notes and *write the Obsidian note from memory, then check.* The RAME structure helps because it forces you to answer specific questions (what do I recognize? what do I assess?) rather than just transcribing. After that, spaced repetition via Anki is the next lever, but only worth building cards once the note is stable. Don't make flashcards from a draft.

**When to split vs combine**

The rule is: **split when management diverges, combine when the clinical question is differential.**

- "Benign rashes" as a differential DDx note = keep combined. You're asking "what is this?" and scanning a list.
- Winter itch vs drug rash vs purpura → if they have meaningfully different management, they each get their own note, with the DDx note linking to them.
- A note like "opioid-induced skin reactions" might logically combine pruritus + flushing because both are managed similarly and looked up at the same time.

Practical test: if you'd realistically look up two things simultaneously in a clinical moment, combine. If they'd always be looked up independently, split.
